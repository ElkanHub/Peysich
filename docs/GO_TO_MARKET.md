# SchoolSpec — getting the first schools

Source: Y Combinator, Gustaf Alströmer, *How to Get Your First Customers*
(youtu.be/hyYCn_kAngI). The points below are what the talk says, then what it means
for SchoolSpec in Ghana. The tracker that goes with this is `docs/leads-tracker.xlsx`.

---

## 1 · The points from the talk

1. **A good product does not sell itself.** Startups take off because founders make
   them take off. You recruit the first customers by hand. Writing more code is the
   most common way founders avoid this.
2. **The founder does the sales.** Talking to users and selling are the same coin.
   You cannot know what to build, or what "good" looks like in a salesperson, until
   you have sold it yourself. Do not hire or outsource sales until you can do it.
3. **Sales is the easiest job to learn** if you know the problem, the product and
   the market. In the customer's eyes you are the expert. Caring about their problem
   is infectious.
4. **The Brex example.** First ten customers came from people they already knew.
   The first version was tiny but useful. The founder onboarded every customer
   himself. The email had limited spots, said exactly who it was for, named the one
   thing others could not do, and said the price.
5. **How to write the email.** Six to eight sentences. Plain language, no jargon.
   Name the customer's problem. Plain text, no HTML. Say you are the founder. One or
   two lines of social proof, shown not told. A link to a simple site with
   screenshots. A short video or GIF that gets to the point in seconds. One clear
   ask: a call, a meeting, or a sign-up.
6. **The funnel, in founder words.** Make the list → send the message → run the
   demo → talk price → close → **onboard**. Founders forget the last step, and the
   customer never really starts, and churns.
7. **Track it in a spreadsheet from day one.** School, person, title, phone,
   source, stage, date of each step. Do this yourself; it is the data you will need.
8. **First customers should be the easiest.** People you know beat strangers.
   Small organisations with one decision-maker beat big ones with committees.
   Prioritise those most likely to close; let the slow ones go politely
   ("let's talk again in six months").
9. **Most people are not early adopters.** Most messages get archived, not
   rejected. That is why you send hundreds. You are not converting the majority;
   you are finding the few who try new things.
10. **Charge.** If they are not paying, they are not a customer and you do not have
    a company. Paying is the clearest sign you are delivering value. Do not offer
    free trials or unpaid pilots in B2B. Offer a money-back guarantee or a
    month-by-month opt-out instead. Raise the price until customers complain but
    still pay. If they will not pay, move on; that is information.
11. **Work backwards from the goal.** Example: 500 messages → 50% opened → 5%
    reply (~20) → 50% take a demo (10) → 20% close = **2 customers**. Send 100
    messages with those rates and you get zero, and wrongly conclude "sales does
    not work for me". You cannot close 5 from 10 leads. Volume first, then learn
    your own rates.
12. **Qualify on the first call.** Ask questions up front. Founders run the demos
    because they know the pain points.
13. **Scalable channels come later.** SEO, ads and referrals are how companies grow
    at scale, not how they start. Airbnb's first 2,000 customers did not come from
    a referral program. Start where you can talk to people.
14. **Tools:** a simple CRM (Pipedrive, Close, Apollo), Hunter for contacts. Book:
    *Founding Sales*. Newsletter: Lenny's.

## 2 · What it means for SchoolSpec

**Your "startups" are private basic schools with an owner who is also the head.**
One person decides, the same day, in person. That is the easy segment. Mission and
big private schools with boards and IT committees are the hard segment; leave them
for later.

**Your channel is WhatsApp and the compound gate, not email.** Proprietors read
WhatsApp; they do not read email. The talk's email rules still apply word for word
to a WhatsApp message, and it will be even shorter.

**Your demo is on their phone, with their real school.** Ask for their class list
(any exercise book or Excel sheet). Import it. Show the register marked for one of
their classes on their own phone in under a minute. Show a report card with their
crest. The point of "the 30-second register" only lands when it is their children.

**Onboard in person, every one, until you have ten.** "Set up and live in under an
hour" is your promise on the landing page; make it a visit. Sit in the office, do
the six setup cards with them, mark the first register with a teacher. Do not leave
until a real register has been saved.

**Charge from the first school.** The site offers 14 days free. The talk says B2B
free trials are a mistake. Decision for you, but the recommendation is: keep the
14 days as the *setup period*, take the first month's GHS 99 by MoMo on the day
you onboard, and promise the money back within 30 days if they stop. A school that
will not pay GHS 99 is telling you something; thank them and move on.

**Qualify in the first five minutes:** How many pupils? Who marks the register
today? How do parents pay fees now? Who prints report cards, and how long does it
take? Do teachers have smartphones? Who decides on spending, and is it you? A "no"
on the last one means a longer road; note it and prioritise elsewhere.

**Social proof you already have:** the demo school runs live; the landing page and
the app are real; you built it for GES structure (Creche to JHS 3, A1–F9). After the
first school, its head's name and a WhatsApp voice note from her are worth more
than any feature.

**The offer for the first ten** (limited spots, like Brex):
> "I'm opening SchoolSpec to 10 schools this term. I set it up with you in person
> in one hour, you keep your first month free if it does not save your teachers
> time, and you get my number for anything."

**The numbers.** Aim for 10 paying schools by the end of the term. With warm
introductions the rates are better than the talk's cold example, but plan on the
cold numbers and be pleased if you beat them. The `Funnel` sheet in the tracker
does the arithmetic; change the blue cells to your own rates as you learn them.

## 3 · Where the first hundred names come from

Ordered easiest first, as the talk says.

1. **People who already know you.** Relatives who own or run schools. Your own old
   schools. Teachers you know who can name their proprietor. Church members who run
   schools. Ask each for two introductions.
2. **The website's own lead form and plan requests.** Console → Leads and Console →
   Requests. Anyone there raised a hand; call them the same day.
3. **Every private basic school within reach of where you live.** Walk in during
   break time with the app on your phone. Ask for the proprietor. Ten schools a
   morning.
4. **Proprietors' associations.** GNAPS and GNACOPS regional and district chapters
   hold meetings; ask a member to bring you, or to forward one WhatsApp message to
   the group.
5. **Suppliers who already sell to schools.** The printer who does their report
   cards, the exercise-book wholesaler, the MoMo agent by the gate. They know every
   proprietor and are trusted; offer them a finder's month.
6. **Teachers' WhatsApp groups and Facebook groups for proprietors.** One plain
   message with a 20-second screen recording of the register. Not a poster.
7. **Churches and mosques with basic schools attached.** One decision-maker, often
   reachable through the congregation.

## 4 · Messages to copy

**WhatsApp, first contact (from you, to a proprietor you were introduced to):**
> Good morning Madam Adjei, this is Elkanah. [Name] gave me your number. I built
> SchoolSpec: your teachers mark the register on their phone in 30 seconds and
> parents of absent children get an SMS before first period; report cards print
> under your crest in one click; fees are recorded and receipted with an SMS to the
> parent. It is GHS 99 a month. I am taking 10 schools this term and I set each one
> up in person in an hour. Can I come by on Thursday morning and show you on your
> own class list? — schoolspec.com

**After the demo, same day:**
> Thank you for the time this morning. As we agreed, I will come on [day] at [time]
> to set up [school] and mark the first register with [teacher]. The first month is
> GHS 99 by MoMo to [number]; if it does not save your teachers time in 30 days, I
> return it. Please send me the class list in any form before then.

**The polite let-go:**
> It has been good getting to know [school]. It sounds like the timing is not right
> this term; may I check in with you at the start of next term?

---

## 5 · The weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** add 25 names to the tracker. Send 25 messages or make 25 calls.
- **Tuesday to Thursday:** demos and setup visits. Two a day is a full day.
- **Friday:** update every row's stage; count the funnel; note what people said
  "no" to; fix the one thing that came up most.
- **Every day:** check Console → Leads.

Do this for six weeks before deciding anything about "whether sales works".
