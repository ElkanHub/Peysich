## One country, sixteen regions

Ghana is one country, but it is big. From the beach at Accra to the border at Paga in the far north is more than 800 kilometres. That is a long trotro ride!

To make the country easier to run, Ghana is divided into **sixteen regions**. Each region has a **capital town** where its government offices are.

For many years Ghana had ten regions. In **2019**, six new regions were created, making sixteen.

In this book you will learn all sixteen regions and their capitals, find them on the map, learn one special thing about each, and see how a region is governed.

## The map of Ghana

Look at the map. Ghana is shaped a bit like a rectangle standing up.

- The **south** touches the sea, the Gulf of Guinea.
- The **north** touches Burkina Faso.
- The **west** touches Côte d'Ivoire (Ivory Coast).
- The **east** touches Togo.

The big blue shape running down the east side is **Lake Volta**.

Each coloured shape on the map is a region. The dot inside each region is its capital.

## The coast: four regions by the sea

Four regions touch the sea: **Western, Central, Greater Accra** and **Volta**.

Life by the sea is special. There are:
- **Fishing** towns with canoes painted in bright colours.
- **Harbours** at Tema and Takoradi where big ships bring goods in and take cocoa and oil out.
- **Castles and forts** built hundreds of years ago by Europeans.
- **Beaches** where people relax at the weekend.

The sea also brings the moist winds that give the south its two rainy seasons.

**Try this:** Which of the four coast regions is furthest west? Which is furthest east?

## Greater Accra Region

**Capital: Accra**

Greater Accra is the **smallest** region in size but has the **most people**. Accra is also the **capital city of the whole country**.

Here you find:
- The **Jubilee House**, where the President works.
- **Parliament**, where laws are made.
- **Independence Square** and the **Black Star Gate**, where Ghana celebrates Independence Day on 6 March.
- **Kotoka International Airport**.
- **Tema**, Ghana's biggest harbour.
- **Makola Market**, one of the busiest markets in West Africa.

The Ga people are the first people of Accra. Their big festival is **Homowo**, which celebrates the end of hunger.

## Central Region

**Capital: Cape Coast**

Central Region is famous for **history** and **schools**.

**Cape Coast Castle** and **Elmina Castle** are old forts by the sea. Hundreds of years ago, enslaved Africans were held there before being taken across the ocean. Today people from all over the world visit to remember.

Cape Coast has some of Ghana's oldest and best secondary schools, so people call it the "centre of education".

**Kakum National Park** has a rope walkway high up in the trees of the rainforest.

The Fante people live here. Their big festival is **Fetu Afahye** in Cape Coast.

**Try this:** Why do you think the Europeans built castles right next to the sea?

## Western Region

**Capital: Sekondi-Takoradi**

Sekondi-Takoradi is really two towns joined together. People often just say "Takoradi" or "Tadi".

Western Region is called the **oil city** region. In 2007, oil was found under the sea off the coast. Now ships and helicopters go out to the oil rigs from Takoradi.

The region also has:
- Ghana's **wettest** place, near Axim.
- **Nzulezu**, a village built on stilts over a lake.
- Beautiful beaches at **Busua** and **Axim**.
- **Takoradi Harbour**, built in 1928, Ghana's first deep-sea harbour.

**Try this:** What do you think is carried in the big ships at Takoradi Harbour?

## Western North Region

**Capital: Sefwi Wiawso**

Western North is one of the **six new regions** made in 2019. It was cut from the top part of the old Western Region.

It is a region of **forests and cocoa farms**. The land is green, the rain is plentiful and the soil is rich. Much of Ghana's cocoa, which is made into chocolate all over the world, comes from here and from neighbouring regions.

The Sefwi people are the main group. Their towns include Sefwi Wiawso, Bibiani and Juaboso.

**Bibiani** is also known for gold mining.

**Try this:** Cocoa beans grow in a pod on a tree. Have you ever seen a cocoa pod? What colour is it?

## Eastern Region

**Capital: Koforidua**

Eastern Region sits just north of Accra, and it is full of **hills, waterfalls and the big dam**.

- The **Akosombo Dam** on the Volta River makes most of Ghana's hydroelectricity.
- **Boti Falls** is a twin waterfall that people visit in the rainy season.
- The **Aburi Botanical Gardens** on the hills above Accra are cool and green.
- Koforidua is famous for its **bead market**. Beads are made from recycled glass.

The region grows cocoa, palm oil and the pineapples sold along the Accra road.

**Try this:** Why do you think Aburi, on a hill, is cooler than Accra?

## Volta Region

**Capital: Ho**

Volta Region runs along the eastern border with Togo. It is named after the **Volta River** and **Lake Volta**.

It has Ghana's **highest mountain**, **Mount Afadja** (often called Afadjato), near the village of Liati Wote. Many people climb it for the view.

Other famous places:
- **Wli Falls**, Ghana's tallest waterfall.
- **Keta** and **Anloga**, fishing and salt-making towns by the sea.
- **Hohoe** and **Kpando** in the hills.

The Ewe people are the main group. Their big festival is **Hogbetsotso**, which remembers a long journey their ancestors made.

**Try this:** Volta Region touches both the sea and Lake Volta. Find both on the map.
===
## Oti Region

**Capital: Dambai**

Oti Region is a **new region** from 2019. It was the northern part of the old Volta Region.

It is named after the **Oti River**, which flows into Lake Volta.

Dambai sits right on the lake. For a long time, people crossed the lake here by ferry. Now there is a **bridge** at Dambai so cars and lorries can cross.

The region is home to many groups, including the Guan, Konkomba and Akan. Farmers grow yam, maize and rice, and people fish in the lake.

**Try this:** Why would a bridge be better than a ferry for a town like Dambai?
===
## Ashanti Region

**Capital: Kumasi**

Ashanti Region is right in the **middle** of Ghana. Kumasi is Ghana's **second-biggest city**, and people call it the "Garden City".

The Ashanti kingdom is one of the most famous in Africa. Its king is the **Asantehene**, who lives at **Manhyia Palace** in Kumasi. The **Golden Stool** is the symbol of the kingdom.

Kumasi also has:
- **Kejetia Market**, one of the biggest markets in West Africa.
- **Bonwire**, where **kente** cloth is woven.
- **Lake Bosomtwe**, a round lake made by a meteorite long ago.
- **Obuasi**, a gold-mining town.

**Try this:** Kumasi is in the centre of Ghana. Why is that a good place for a big market?
===
## Bono Region

**Capital: Sunyani**

Bono Region was part of the old **Brong-Ahafo Region** until 2019, when Brong-Ahafo was split into three: Bono, Bono East and Ahafo.

Sunyani is a clean, quiet town known for its **Cocoa House**, a tall building that stands out above the town.

The region is famous for:
- Growing **cashew nuts**, cocoa and maize.
- **Fiema and Boabeng**, a village where monkeys are sacred and live freely among the people.
- The **Bono people**, who are among the oldest Akan groups in Ghana.

**Try this:** Why might a community decide to protect monkeys instead of hunting them?
===
## Bono East Region

**Capital: Techiman**

Bono East is another new region from 2019.

Techiman has one of the **largest food markets in Ghana**. Every week, lorries from all over the country come to buy yams, maize, plantain and vegetables. Techiman is in the middle of Ghana, where the forest meets the savannah, so food from both sides arrives there.

Other places:
- **Kintampo Waterfalls**, a famous tourist spot.
- **Yeji**, a fishing town on Lake Volta.
- **Atebubu** and **Nkoranza**, farming towns.

The region is often called the **"food basket"** of Ghana.

**Try this:** Why is a town in the middle of the country a good place for a big food market?
===
## Ahafo Region

**Capital: Goaso**

Ahafo is the **smallest of the three** regions made from Brong-Ahafo.

It is a land of **forest, cocoa and gold**.

- The **Ahafo gold mine** near Kenyasi is one of the biggest in Ghana.
- Cocoa and timber come from its thick forests.
- Towns include Goaso, Hwidiem, Kenyasi, Mim and Bechem.

Goaso is a small, growing town. Becoming a regional capital means new roads, offices and hospitals are being built.

**Try this:** How might life change in a small town when it becomes a regional capital?

## The north: savannah land

Five regions make up the north of Ghana: **Northern, Savannah, North East, Upper East** and **Upper West**.

The land here is **savannah**: wide grassland with scattered trees like shea and baobab. It is hotter and drier than the south, with **one rainy season**.

Life in the north:
- Farmers grow **millet, sorghum, rice, yam and groundnuts**, and keep cattle, goats and sheep.
- Women collect **shea nuts** and make shea butter.
- Many people wear the **smock** (called fugu or batakari), a hand-woven shirt.
- Round houses with thatched roofs are common in villages.

**Try this:** Why do you think people in the north build round houses with thick mud walls?

## Northern Region

**Capital: Tamale**

Tamale is the **biggest city in the north** of Ghana and one of the fastest-growing cities in West Africa.

The Northern Region is the home of the **Dagomba** people. Their king is the **Ya-Na**, who lives at **Yendi**.

Famous places:
- The **Tamale Central Mosque**, a landmark of the city.
- **Yendi**, the seat of the Dagbon kingdom.
- The **Tamale Sports Stadium**.

Tamale is known for its many bicycles and motorbikes, and for shea butter, rice and groundnuts.

**Try this:** Tamale is far from the sea. How do you think goods get there?

## Savannah Region

**Capital: Damongo**

Savannah Region is a new region from 2019, cut from the western part of the old Northern Region. It is Ghana's **largest region** by land, but it has few people.

It is home to **Mole National Park**, Ghana's biggest wildlife park. There you can see **elephants, antelopes, warthogs, baboons** and many birds.

Next to Mole is **Larabanga**, which has one of the **oldest mosques in West Africa**, built of mud in the old Sudanese style.

The Gonja people are the main group. Their king is the **Yagbonwura**.

## North East Region

**Capital: Nalerigu**

North East Region is another new region from 2019, cut from the Northern Region.

Nalerigu is the home of the **Nayiri**, king of the **Mamprusi** people. The Mamprusi kingdom is very old, and the Dagomba and Mossi kingdoms grew from it.

Near Nalerigu is the **Nalerigu Defence Wall**, built hundreds of years ago to protect the town. People say it was built with mud mixed with honey.

Other towns include **Walewale** and **Gambaga**, which sits on a high escarpment with cool breezes.

Farmers here grow millet, maize and rice, and the region is known for its smocks.

**Try this:** Why would a town build a wall around itself long ago?

## Upper East Region

**Capital: Bolgatanga**

Upper East is in the far north-east corner of Ghana, touching Burkina Faso and Togo. People shorten Bolgatanga to **"Bolga"**.

Bolga is famous for its **baskets**. Women weave strong, colourful baskets from straw, and they are sold all over the world.

Other famous places:
- **Paga**, right on the border, where there is a pond with **friendly crocodiles** that let people sit near them.
- **Tongo Hills** and the shrines of Tengzug.
- **Navrongo**, with its mud-built cathedral.

The Frafra, Kusasi and Kassena peoples live here. Houses are painted with beautiful patterns.

**Try this:** Why might crocodiles in one pond be friendly when crocodiles elsewhere are dangerous?

## Upper West Region

**Capital: Wa**

Upper West is in the far north-west corner, touching Burkina Faso and Côte d'Ivoire.

Wa is the home of the **Wa Naa**, the chief of the **Wala** people. His palace is built in the old mud style with pointed towers.

The region is known for:
- **Wechiau Hippo Sanctuary** on the Black Volta River, where hippos live.
- **Gwollu**, which has an old defence wall like Nalerigu's.
- **Shea butter** and groundnut farming.
- The **xylophone** (gyil), a wooden instrument played by the Dagaaba and Sisaala peoples.

It is one of the quietest regions, with wide open spaces.

**Try this:** Hippos live in rivers. Why does the Black Volta make a good home for them?

## Old regions and new regions

Before 2019, Ghana had **ten** regions. Then the government asked the people in some places if they wanted new regions. They voted **yes**.

Here is what changed:

| Old region | Became |
|---|---|
| Western | Western + **Western North** |
| Volta | Volta + **Oti** |
| Northern | Northern + **Savannah** + **North East** |
| Brong-Ahafo | **Bono** + **Bono East** + **Ahafo** |

The other six regions (Greater Accra, Central, Eastern, Ashanti, Upper East, Upper West) stayed the same.

Why make new regions? So that government offices are **closer to the people**, and so that places far from the old capital get more attention.

**Try this:** Which old region was split into the most pieces?

## Regions, districts and communities

A region is divided into smaller parts called **districts**. Ghana has more than **260 districts**.

Think of it like a school:
- The **country** is the whole school.
- A **region** is like a class level, such as Basic 4.
- A **district** is like one classroom.
- Your **community** (town or village) is like your row of desks.

Big districts with cities are called **Metropolitan** (like Accra or Kumasi). Medium ones are **Municipal**. Smaller ones are just **District**.

Each district has an **Assembly** that looks after local things: markets, refuse collection, local roads, basic schools and clinics.

**Try this:** What is the name of your district? Where is its office?
===
## Who runs a region?

Every region has a **Regional Minister**. The President chooses the Regional Minister, and Parliament must agree.

The Regional Minister:
- Represents the President in the region.
- Chairs the **Regional Coordinating Council**, which brings together the leaders of all the districts.
- Makes sure government plans (roads, hospitals, schools) are carried out.
- Helps keep peace and order.

The Regional Minister works from the **Regional Coordinating Council** office in the regional capital. That is one reason the capital town matters: it is where the region's government sits.

**Try this:** Find out the name of your Regional Minister. Listen for it on the news.
===
## Who runs a district?

Each district is run by a **District Chief Executive** (DCE). In a city it is a **Metropolitan Chief Executive** (MCE), and in a municipality a **Municipal Chief Executive** (MCE). People often just say "the DCE".

The DCE works with the **District Assembly**. Assembly members are mostly **elected by the people** in each small area, called an **electoral area**.

The Assembly decides on:
- Where to build a new market or clinic.
- How to collect rubbish.
- Local rules, called by-laws.
- How to spend the district's money.

Below the Assembly are **Unit Committees** in each community, the closest level of government to you.

**Try this:** If you could ask your Assembly member for one thing for your community, what would it be?

## Chiefs and traditional leaders

Alongside the government, Ghana's regions have **chiefs**. Chiefs are traditional leaders chosen according to the customs of their people.

Each region has a **Regional House of Chiefs**, and the whole country has a **National House of Chiefs**.

Some of the best-known chiefs:
- The **Asantehene** in Kumasi (Ashanti).
- The **Ya-Na** in Yendi (Northern).
- The **Nayiri** in Nalerigu (North East).
- The **Yagbonwura** in Damongo (Savannah).
- The **Wa Naa** in Wa (Upper West).
- The **Ga Mantse** in Accra (Greater Accra).

Chiefs settle disputes, look after land and customs, and lead festivals. They work together with the government.

**Try this:** Who is the chief of your town? What title does he or she use?

## Festivals of the regions

Every region has festivals that celebrate harvest, history or the ancestors.

- **Homowo** (Greater Accra): the Ga people sprinkle kpokpoi and "hoot at hunger".
- **Fetu Afahye** (Central): Cape Coast's colourful procession.
- **Odwira** (Eastern): the Akuapem people at Akropong.
- **Akwasidae** (Ashanti): the Asantehene sits in state at Manhyia.
- **Hogbetsotso** (Volta): the Anlo-Ewe remember their journey.
- **Damba** (Northern, North East, Savannah): drumming, horses and smocks.
- **Kundum** (Western): the Nzema and Ahanta harvest festival.
- **Bugum** (fire festival) in the north.

**Try this:** Which festival is held where you live? What do people do during it?

## Languages across the regions

Ghana's official language is **English**, but more than **70 languages** are spoken across the regions.

- **Akan** (Twi, Fante): Ashanti, Eastern, Central, Bono, Bono East, Ahafo, Western, Western North.
- **Ga and Dangme**: Greater Accra.
- **Ewe**: Volta and Oti.
- **Dagbani**: Northern.
- **Gonja**: Savannah.
- **Mampruli**: North East.
- **Frafra (Gurenɛ), Kusaal, Kasem**: Upper East.
- **Dagaare, Waali, Sisaali**: Upper West.
- **Nzema**: Western.
- **Hausa** is spoken in zongo communities everywhere.

Many Ghanaians speak three or four languages!

## What the regions make and grow

Each region is known for different things.

- **Cocoa**: Ashanti, Western North, Western, Eastern, Ahafo, Central.
- **Gold**: Ashanti (Obuasi), Western (Tarkwa), Ahafo (Kenyasi), Western North (Bibiani).
- **Oil**: off the coast of Western Region.
- **Fish**: all four coast regions, plus Lake Volta towns in Volta, Oti and Bono East.
- **Yam and maize**: Bono East, Northern, Savannah, Oti.
- **Rice**: Northern, Upper East, Volta.
- **Shea butter**: Northern, Upper East, Upper West, Savannah.
- **Pineapple**: Central, Eastern, Greater Accra.
- **Cashew**: Bono, Bono East.
- **Salt**: Volta (Keta), Greater Accra (Ada).

## Travelling between regions

How do people and goods move between the regions?

- **Roads**: the Accra–Kumasi road, the Accra–Cape Coast–Takoradi coastal road, and the Kumasi–Tamale–Bolga road to the north are the busiest. Trotros, buses and lorries use them every day.
- **Air**: there are airports at Accra, Kumasi, Tamale, Takoradi, Sunyani, Ho and Wa.
- **Water**: the ferry on Lake Volta carries people and yams between Akosombo, Yeji and other lake towns.
- **Rail**: old railway lines link Accra, Kumasi and Takoradi, and new lines are being built.

A trip from Accra to Tamale by road takes about **10 to 12 hours**. By plane, about **one hour**.

**Try this:** How would you travel from your region to Kumasi? How long would it take?

## The flag that unites all sixteen

No matter which region you live in, you are a **Ghanaian**, and the same flag flies over you.

The flag was designed by **Theodosia Okoh** in 1957, when Ghana became independent.

- **Red** remembers those who died for freedom.
- **Gold** stands for Ghana's mineral wealth.
- **Green** stands for the forests and farms.
- The **black star** stands for African freedom.

The regions are different in language, food and weather, but the flag, the anthem, the cedi, the national football team and the constitution belong to everyone.

**Try this:** Draw the flag. Which colour stands for the forests of Western North? Which for the gold of Obuasi?

## Reading the map: compass directions

To describe where a region is, use the compass: **North, South, East, West**.

- Upper West is in the **north-west**.
- Upper East is in the **north-east**.
- Western is in the **south-west**.
- Volta is in the **south-east**.
- Ashanti is in the **centre**.

You can also say which regions **border** (touch) each other.
- Ashanti borders Eastern, Central, Western, Western North, Ahafo, Bono and Bono East.
- Greater Accra borders Central, Eastern and Volta.

**Try this:** Use the map. Which regions border Northern Region? Which region borders the most other regions?

## Tricks for remembering

Here are some ways to remember the capitals.

**Same first letter:**
- **A**ccra for Greater **A**ccra.
- **W**a for Upper **W**est.

**Rhyme or sound:**
- **Ho** is short, like "Volta, Ho!"

**The three B's:** Bono (Sunyani), Bono East (Techiman), Ahafo (Goaso) all came from Brong-Ahafo.

**The far north four:** Wa (west), Bolga (east), Nalerigu (middle-east), Damongo (middle-west), with Tamale in the centre.

## Words to remember

- **Region:** one of the sixteen big parts Ghana is divided into.
- **Regional capital:** the town where the region's government offices are.
- **District:** a smaller part of a region. Ghana has more than 260.
- **Regional Minister:** the person the President chooses to lead a region.
- **District Chief Executive (DCE):** the person who leads a district.
- **District Assembly:** elected people who decide local matters.
- **Chief:** a traditional leader chosen by custom.
- **Savannah:** grassland with scattered trees, found in the north.
- **Border:** the line where one region (or country) meets another.
- **Capital city:** Accra, the capital of the whole country.
