## What is a sentence?

A **sentence** is a group of words that tells us something.

> Ama eats kenkey.

That is a sentence. It tells us what Ama does.

> eats kenkey

That is **not** a sentence. We do not know who eats the kenkey.

A sentence makes sense on its own. When you read it, you understand it.

**Try this:** Which of these is a sentence?

- Kofi runs to school.
- runs to school

## Every sentence starts with a capital letter

The first letter of a sentence is always a **capital letter**. It is a big letter.

> **T**he goat is black.
> **M**y name is Abena.
> **W**e go to school.

Small letters: a b c d e f g

Capital letters: A B C D E F G

**Try this:** Point to the capital letter at the start of each sentence.

- Kwame likes rice.
- The sun is hot.
- I am six years old.
===
## Small letters and capital letters

Every letter has two shapes: a **small** one and a **capital** one.

| Small | Capital |
|---|---|
| a | A |
| b | B |
| d | D |
| g | G |
| k | K |
| m | M |
| t | T |

Some capitals look like the small letter, only bigger: **c C**, **o O**, **s S**.

Some look different: **a A**, **e E**, **g G**, **r R**.

**Try this:** Write your name. Which letter is the capital? It is the first one!
===
## Every sentence ends with a full stop

A **full stop** is a small dot: **.**

It goes at the end of a sentence. It tells the reader: *stop here, the sentence is finished*.

> Ama has a red dress**.**
> The dog barks**.**
> I like my school**.**

Without a full stop, the reader does not know where to stop.

**Try this:** Put a full stop at the end of each sentence.

- Kofi has a ball
- The hen lays eggs
- We eat banku

## Capital letter at the start, full stop at the end

Here is the rule. Say it with me:

> **Capital letter at the start,**
> **full stop at the end.**

Look at this sentence:

> **T**he cat sleeps on the mat**.**

Capital **T** at the start. Full stop at the end. Perfect!

**Try this:** Fix these sentences. Add a capital letter and a full stop.

- the goat eats grass
- my father has a car
- abena sings a song

## Spaces between words

When we write, we leave a **space** between each word. The space is like a little gap.

Look:

> Kofi likes mangoes.

Now look without spaces:

> Kofilikesmangoes.

That is hard to read! Spaces help the reader.

**Tip:** Put your finger on the page after each word. Start the next word after your finger. This is called a **finger space**.

**Try this:** Write this sentence with finger spaces:

> I am happy.
===
## Naming words

A **naming word** is a word for a person, an animal, a place or a thing.

**People:** teacher, mother, boy, girl, Kofi

**Animals:** goat, hen, dog, fish

**Places:** school, market, church, Accra

**Things:** ball, book, pencil, trotro

We also call naming words **nouns**.

**Try this:** Find the naming words in this sentence:

> The **girl** has a **book**.

Girl is a person. Book is a thing. Both are naming words.

Now find the naming words here: *The goat is in the market.* Goat is an animal. Market is a place.

## Names of people and places

The names of **people** and **places** always start with a capital letter. Even in the middle of a sentence!

> My friend is **A**ma.
> We live in **K**umasi.
> **K**ofi goes to **T**ema.

Your own name starts with a capital letter. Your town's name does too. **Ghana** starts with a capital **G**.

**Try this:** Write the name of:

- yourself
- your best friend
- your town or village

Check: did you start each one with a capital letter?

## Doing words

A **doing word** tells us what someone or something **does**.

- run
- jump
- eat
- sing
- read
- sleep
- wash
- cook

We also call doing words **verbs**.

> Kofi **runs**.
> Ama **sings**.
> The dog **sleeps**.

**Try this:** Stand up. Your teacher says a doing word. You do it!

- Jump!
- Clap!
- Sit!
- Wave!

Every one of those is a doing word.

## Find the doing word

Read each sentence. Find the doing word. What is the person or animal doing?

> The hen **lays** eggs.
> Mother **cooks** jollof.
> Kwame **kicks** the ball.
> The baby **cries**.
> We **wash** our hands.

**Try this:** Say what you do at these times. Use a doing word.

- In the morning, I ...
- At school, I ...
- At home, I ...
- At night, I ...
===
## Naming word plus doing word

A simple sentence has a **naming word** and a **doing word**.

| Naming word | Doing word |
|---|---|
| Ama | sings. |
| The goat | runs. |
| Birds | fly. |
| Father | works. |

Put them together:

> **Ama** sings.
> **The goat** runs.

Each is a full sentence. It has a capital letter, a naming word, a doing word and a full stop.

**Try this:** Make sentences. Pick one naming word and one doing word.

Naming: Kofi, the dog, my sister
Doing: jumps, eats, sleeps
===
## Making the sentence longer

We can add more words to say more.

> Ama sings.
> Ama sings **a song**.
> Ama sings a song **at church**.

Each sentence is right. The longer ones tell us more.

> Kofi eats.
> Kofi eats **rice**.
> Kofi eats rice **with his mother**.

**Try this:** Make these longer. Add what, where or who.

- The dog runs.
- I read.
- Abena plays.
===
## "I can" sentences

Here is a special kind of sentence. It starts with **I can**.

> I can run.
> I can jump.
> I can count to twenty.
> I can write my name.

The word **I** is always a capital letter. Always! Even in the middle of a sentence.

**Try this:** Say three things you can do. Start with "I can".

> I can ...
> I can ...
> I can ...
===
## Write your "I can" sentences

Now write them down. Remember the rules:

1. Capital **I** at the start.
2. A finger space between each word.
3. A doing word after **can**.
4. A full stop at the end.

Kwame wrote:

> I can swim.
> I can sweep the room.
> I can help my mother.

**Try this:** Write three "I can" sentences in your exercise book. Draw a picture next to each one.

## Put the words in order

The words in a sentence must be in the right **order**.

> goat The eats grass.

That is mixed up! Put it right:

> The goat eats grass.

**Try this:** Put these words in the right order. Remember the capital letter and full stop.

- ball a has Kofi
- school to go We
- mangoes likes Ama
- hot is sun The
===
## Find the mistake

Abena wrote some sentences. Each one has a mistake. Can you find it?

1. the hen is brown.
   *(No capital letter at the start.)*

2. My bag is red
   *(No full stop at the end.)*

3. i can jump.
   *(The word I must be a capital letter.)*

4. We live in accra.
   *(Accra is a place. It needs a capital letter.)*

**Try this:** Write all four sentences the correct way.

## A question is a sentence too

Some sentences **ask** something. They end with a **question mark**: **?**

> What is your name**?**
> Where is Kofi**?**
> Do you like rice**?**

A question still starts with a capital letter. But instead of a full stop, it ends with a question mark.

**Try this:** Which of these is a question?

- I like fish.
- Do you like fish?

Ask a friend a question. Then write it down with a question mark. Remember: capital letter at the start, question mark at the end.
===
## Reading your sentence aloud

After you write a sentence, **read it aloud**. Your ears will tell you if something is wrong.

Read this:

> Kofi the ball.

Something is missing! There is no doing word. Fix it:

> Kofi kicks the ball.

Read this:

> Ama runs.

Good. It makes sense.

**Try this:** Write a sentence. Read it aloud to a friend. Ask: "Does it make sense?"
===
## Writing neatly

Neat writing is easy to read.

- Sit up straight.
- Hold your pencil gently, not too tight.
- Make your capital letters tall.
- Make your small letters small.
- Keep your letters on the line.
- Leave finger spaces.

Look at Kwame's writing:

> I can read.

The capital **I** is tall. The small letters sit on the line. There are spaces. There is a full stop.

**Try this:** Copy "I can read." as neatly as you can.

## Two sentences together

You can write two sentences, one after the other. Each one gets its own capital letter and full stop.

> Kofi has a dog. The dog is brown.

Two sentences! See the full stop after **dog**, and the capital **T** after it.

> I went to the market. I bought an orange.

**Try this:** Write two sentences about what you did yesterday. Read them aloud. Can you hear where the first one stops?

## Sentence check-list

Before you say "I am finished", check your sentence:

1. Does it start with a **capital letter**?
2. Does it have a **naming word** (who or what)?
3. Does it have a **doing word** (what they do)?
4. Are there **spaces** between the words?
5. Does it end with a **full stop** (or a question mark)?
6. Does it **make sense** when you read it aloud?

If you can say **yes** to all six, your sentence is excellent!

**Try this:** Check this sentence: *the goat jump.* How many things need fixing?

## What we have learnt

Well done! You can now:

- Say what a **sentence** is.
- Start a sentence with a **capital letter**.
- End a sentence with a **full stop**.
- Leave **finger spaces** between words.
- Find **naming words** (people, animals, places, things).
- Find **doing words** (run, eat, sing).
- Write **"I can"**, **"I am"** and **"I have"** sentences.
- Use a capital letter for **I** and for names.
- **Check** your sentence before you finish.

Keep writing every day!
