## Words have jobs

Every word in a sentence has a job to do. Some words **name** things. Some words tell us what is **happening**.

Read this sentence: *Ama eats kenkey.*

*Ama* names a girl. *Kenkey* names a food. *Eats* tells us what Ama is doing.

Words that name are called **nouns**. Words that tell us what is happening are called **verbs**.

In this book you will learn to spot nouns and verbs, change one thing into many things, and match nouns and verbs so your sentences sound right.

Let's start with naming words.
===
## Naming words: nouns

A **noun** is a naming word. It names a person, a place, an animal or a thing.

- A person: *teacher, mother, Kofi*
- A place: *school, market, Kumasi*
- An animal: *goat, hen, lizard*
- A thing: *ball, book, trotro*

Look around you right now. Everything you can point to has a name. All of those names are nouns.

**Try this:** Point to five things in the room and say their names. You have just said five nouns!
===
## People are nouns

The names of people are nouns. So are the words for what people do or who they are to us.

- *girl, boy, man, woman*
- *teacher, nurse, driver, farmer, trader*
- *mother, father, sister, brother, grandmother*

The woman in the picture is a **trader**. She sells vegetables at the market. *Trader* is a noun. *Woman* is a noun too.

**Try this:** Write down the nouns for three people in your family and three people at your school.

## Places are nouns

The names of places are nouns.

- *house, kitchen, classroom, church, mosque*
- *market, farm, beach, road, village*
- *Accra, Tamale, Cape Coast, Ghana*

In the picture you can see the entrance to a famous **market** in Accra. *Market* is a noun. *Accra* is a noun. *Entrance* is a noun too!

**Try this:** Name three places you went to this week. Each one is a noun. Say a sentence about each place: *I went to the ___.*

## Things are nouns

The names of things are nouns. Things can be big or small, old or new.

- *cup, spoon, bowl, pot*
- *pencil, book, bag, chair*
- *kenkey, banku, rice, orange*
- *bicycle, phone, radio*

Look at the picture. Can you name the things you see? *Kenkey, fish, pepper, plate.* Four nouns on one plate!

**Try this:** Empty your school bag. Say the noun for everything inside it. How many nouns did you find?

## Animals are nouns

The names of animals are nouns too.

- *goat, sheep, cow, dog, cat*
- *hen, cock, duck, bird*
- *lizard, snake, frog, fish*
- *ant, mosquito, butterfly*

The animal in the picture is a **goat**. Goats are everywhere in Ghana! The word *goat* is a noun.

**Try this:** Make a list of every animal you can see or hear from your house. Hens? Goats? Lizards on the wall? Each one is a noun.

## Special names: proper nouns

Some nouns name one special person, place or thing. These are called **proper nouns**. They always start with a **capital letter**.

- Names of people: *Ama, Kwame, Mr Mensah*
- Names of places: *Ghana, Accra, Lake Volta*
- Days and months: *Monday, Friday, March*
- Special days: *Christmas, Eid, Independence Day*

*Girl* is an ordinary noun. *Abena* is a proper noun, because it names one special girl.

**Try this:** Write your own name, your town and today's day. Did you use capital letters?

## Worked example: nouns in a sentence

Let us find all the nouns in this sentence together.

*On Saturday, Kwame and his sister sold oranges at the market in Kumasi.*

**Step 1:** Look for people. *Kwame* and *sister*. Two nouns.

**Step 2:** Look for places. *Market* and *Kumasi*. Two more.

**Step 3:** Look for things. *Oranges*. One more.

**Step 4:** Look for special names. *Saturday* is a day. It is a proper noun.

That is six nouns in one sentence! Notice *Kwame*, *Kumasi* and *Saturday* start with capital letters because they are proper nouns.
===
## One or many

A noun can name **one** thing or **more than one** thing.

- One **goat**. Many **goats**.
- One **book**. Many **books**.
- One **girl**. Many **girls**.

When a noun names one thing, we say it is **singular**. When it names more than one, we say it is **plural**.

The word *singular* sounds like *single*: just one. The word *plural* sounds like *plus*: more.

**Try this:** Look at your hands. One hand is singular. Two hands are plural! Now say the singular and plural for *foot*. Careful, it is a tricky one. We will meet it soon.
===
## Adding -s

For most nouns, we make the plural by adding **-s** to the end.

| One | Many |
|-----|------|
| cup | cups |
| pencil | pencils |
| trotro | trotros |
| mango | mangoes |

Wait, *mango* became *mangoes*! Some words that end in **-o** add **-es**. We will learn more about -es on the next page.

**Try this:** Write the plural of these nouns: *bag, chair, teacher, spoon, hen, road*.

Kofi wrote: *bags, chairs, teachers, spoons, hens, roads.* Did you get the same?
===
## Adding -es

If a noun ends in **-s, -x, -ch** or **-sh**, we add **-es** to make the plural. Say the word out loud. You will hear the extra sound.

| One | Many |
|-----|------|
| bus | buses |
| box | boxes |
| church | churches |
| dish | dishes |
| glass | glasses |

Try saying "one bus, two buss". It does not sound right! That is why we need the **-es**. "Two bus-es." Now it sounds right.

**Try this:** Write the plural of *bench, fox, brush, class*.
===
## Tricky plurals

Some nouns do not follow the rules. They change in a special way. You just have to learn them.

| One | Many |
|-----|------|
| child | children |
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| foot | feet |
| tooth | teeth |
| mouse | mice |

And a few nouns do not change at all: one **sheep**, many **sheep**. One **fish**, many **fish**.

**Try this:** Fill the gaps. *One child, three ___. One tooth, many ___. One man, two ___.*
===

Did you notice that *is* changed to *are*? We will talk about that soon.

## Doing words: verbs

A **verb** is a doing word. It tells us what someone or something **does**.

- Ama **runs**.
- The boys **play** football.
- The goat **eats** grass.
- Grandmother **sleeps**.

In the picture, the children **play**, **run** and **kick**. Every one of those words is a verb.

A verb is the action in a sentence. If you can do it, it is probably a verb.

**Try this:** Stand up. Jump three times. Clap twice. Sit down. You just did four verbs!

## Verbs you do with your body

Many verbs are things you do with your body. These are easy to spot because you can act them out.

- *run, walk, jump, hop, skip*
- *clap, wave, point, push, pull*
- *eat, drink, chew, bite*
- *sweep, wash, cook, carry, dig*

Kofi **sweeps** the compound. Abena **carries** water. Their father **digs** on the farm.

**Try this:** Choose a verb from the list. Act it out without speaking. Can your friend guess the verb?
===
## Verbs you do quietly

Not every verb is loud or busy. Some verbs happen quietly, inside you or while you sit still.

- *think, dream, wonder*
- *sleep, rest, wait*
- *listen, look, watch*
- *read, write, draw*
- *like, love, want*

Ama **reads** her book. Kwame **thinks** about the answer. The baby **sleeps**.

These are still verbs. They tell us what someone is doing, even if it is a quiet thing.

**Try this:** Which quiet verbs are you doing right now? *Reading? Thinking? Sitting?*

## Worked example: find the verb

Let us find the verb in this sentence.

*The hungry dog chased the goat around the compound.*

**Step 1:** Find the nouns. *Dog, goat, compound.* These are naming words, so they are not the verb.

**Step 2:** Ask: what is happening? What did the dog **do**? The dog **chased**.

**Step 3:** Check. Can you act out *chased*? Yes! Can you act out *hungry*? No, that describes the dog. So *chased* is the verb.

The verb is **chased**.

Tip: Find the nouns first. Then ask what the first noun is doing.
===
## Every sentence needs both

A sentence needs a noun and a verb. Without them, it is not a sentence.

- *Goat.* Just a noun. Not a sentence.
- *Jumps.* Just a verb. Not a sentence.
- *The goat jumps.* A noun and a verb. **A sentence!**

The noun tells us **who** or **what**. The verb tells us what they **do**.

**Try this:** Put a noun and a verb together to make sentences. Use this list.

Nouns: *baby, trotro, Abena, fish*
Verbs: *cries, stops, swims, dances*

*The baby cries. The trotro stops.* Now you finish the rest.
===
## The noun does the verb

In a sentence, the noun that comes first usually does the action.

*Kofi kicks the ball.*

Who kicks? **Kofi**. Kofi is the noun doing the verb. The ball is also a noun, but it is not doing anything. It is being kicked!

*The cat chases the mouse.*

Who chases? **The cat**. The mouse is the one being chased.

**Try this:** In each sentence, say which noun is doing the verb.

1. The teacher rings the bell.
2. Ama waters the plants.
3. The rain wets the road.
===
## One noun, add -s to the verb

Here is something strange but important. When the noun is **one** person or thing, the verb usually ends in **-s**.

- Ama **eats** kenkey.
- The goat **sleeps** under the tree.
- The trotro **stops** at the junction.
- My brother **plays** football.

One Ama, so *eats*. One goat, so *sleeps*.

Say it wrong and listen: *Ama eat kenkey.* It does not sound right. *Ama eats kenkey.* That's better.

**Try this:** Add -s to the verb. *The baby (cry). Kofi (wash) the plates. The hen (lay) an egg.*
===
## Many nouns, no -s on the verb

When the noun is **more than one**, the verb does **not** end in -s.

- The girls **eat** kenkey.
- The goats **sleep** under the tree.
- The trotros **stop** at the junction.
- My brothers **play** football.

It is like a see-saw. If the noun has the -s (plural), the verb does not. If the noun has no -s (singular), the verb gets it.

- One **girl eats**. Many **girls eat**.

**Try this:** Change these to many. *The dog barks. The bird sings. The child laughs.*
===
## Worked example: match them

Abena must choose the right verb: *The boys (run / runs) to school.*

**Step 1:** Find the noun doing the action. *Boys.*

**Step 2:** Is it one or many? *Boys* ends in -s. It is many.

**Step 3:** Many nouns take the verb **without** -s. So the answer is **run**.

*The boys run to school.*

Now a second one: *The boy (run / runs) to school.*

*Boy* is one. One noun takes the verb **with** -s. *The boy runs to school.*

The noun and the verb must **agree**. That is the rule.
===

## Is and are

*Is* and *are* are special verbs. They do not show an action. They tell us what something **is like** or **where** it is.

- One thing: use **is**. *The trotro is full.*
- Many things: use **are**. *The trotros are full.*

More examples:

- *The mango is ripe. The mangoes are ripe.*
- *My sister is at home. My sisters are at home.*

**Try this:** Choose is or are. *The goat ___ hungry. The children ___ happy. The road ___ wet.*

## Today and yesterday

Verbs can change to show **when** something happened.

Something happening now or every day: *Ama walks to school.*

Something that happened before: *Yesterday, Ama walked to school.*

For many verbs, we add **-ed** to show it already happened.

- play → play**ed**
- cook → cook**ed**
- jump → jump**ed**
- wash → wash**ed**

Some verbs change in a different way: *eat* → *ate*, *run* → *ran*, *go* → *went*. You will learn more of these in Basic 3.

**Try this:** Say what you did yesterday using three -ed verbs.
===
## Same word, two jobs

Some words can be a noun **or** a verb. It depends on the job the word is doing in the sentence.

- *Ama drinks **water**.* (*Water* is a thing. Noun.)
- *Ama **waters** the plants.* (*Waters* is an action. Verb.)

- *We watched the **play**.* (A thing you watch. Noun.)
- *We **play** outside.* (An action. Verb.)

Look at the mango tree. *Mango* is a noun. Now: *Kofi **climbs** the mango tree.* *Climbs* is the verb.

**Try this:** Use *fish* as a noun in one sentence and as a verb in another.

## Building a sentence

Let us build sentences step by step. Start with a noun. Add a verb. Then add more.

1. Noun: *The hen*
2. Add a verb: *The hen **pecks**.*
3. Add another noun: *The hen pecks the **corn**.*
4. Add a place: *The hen pecks the corn **in the yard**.*

Every time we add something, the sentence tells us more. But it always needs the noun and the verb in the middle of it all.

**Try this:** Build a sentence in four steps. Start with *The fisherman*. What does he do? What does he do it to? Where?

## The noun and verb game

Here is a game for the whole class.

Make two piles of cards. On the first pile, write nouns: *goat, teacher, trotro, baby, Kwame, rain*. On the second pile, write verbs: *jumps, sings, stops, cries, runs, falls*.

Pick one card from each pile. Read the sentence you have made. *The goat sings! The trotro cries!*

Some sentences will be silly. That is fine. They are still sentences, because each has a noun and a verb.

**Try this:** Which silly sentence made you laugh the most? Draw a picture of it.

## Mistakes to watch for

**Forgetting capital letters on proper nouns.** Write *Ama* and *Ghana*, not *ama* and *ghana*.

**Adding -s to the wrong word.** *The goats eats* is wrong. If the noun is plural, the verb has no -s. *The goats eat.*

**Guessing tricky plurals.** It is *children*, not *childs*. It is *feet*, not *foots*.

**Mixing up is and are.** One thing *is*. Many things *are*.

**Leaving out the verb.** *The dog the ball.* What did the dog do? Add a verb: *The dog chased the ball.*

**Try this:** Find three mistakes. *the childs is playing with ball.*
===
## What we learned

Well done! Here is everything from this book.

- A **noun** names a person, place, animal or thing.
- **Proper nouns** name one special person or place and start with a capital letter.
- **Singular** means one. **Plural** means more than one.
- Most plurals add **-s**. Words ending in -s, -x, -ch, -sh add **-es**. Some are tricky: *children, feet, men*.
- A **verb** is a doing word.
- Every sentence needs a noun and a verb.
- One noun → verb with -s. Many nouns → verb without -s.
- One thing **is**. Many things **are**.

Keep hunting for nouns and verbs in every sentence you read!
