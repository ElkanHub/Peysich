## What is a verb?

A **verb** is a doing word or a being word. It tells us what someone **does** or what someone **is**.

- Ama **runs** to school.
- Kofi **eats** kenkey.
- Abena **is** happy.
- The trotro **stops** at the junction.

Every sentence needs a verb. Without a verb, it is not a sentence. "Kofi to the market" is not a sentence. "Kofi **walks** to the market" is.

**Try this:** Find the verb in each sentence.
1. The teacher writes on the board.
2. We play football after school.
3. My mother sells tomatoes.
4. The dog barks at night.
===
## What is tense?

**Tense** tells us **when** something happens.

There are three main tenses:

- **Past**: it already happened. *Yesterday Kofi **walked** to school.*
- **Present**: it is happening now, or it happens every day. *Today Kofi **walks** to school.*
- **Future**: it has not happened yet. *Tomorrow Kofi **will walk** to school.*

The verb changes its shape to show the time. The verb is like a clock inside the sentence.

**Try this:** Say three things about yourself: one that happened yesterday, one that happens every day, and one that will happen tomorrow.

## Time words

Some words tell us the tense before we even see the verb. We call them **time words**.

**Past:** yesterday, last week, last year, ago, this morning (if it is now afternoon), when I was small.

**Present:** now, today, every day, always, usually, often, at the moment.

**Future:** tomorrow, next week, next year, soon, later, in two days.

- **Yesterday** we visited Grandma. (past)
- We **always** sweep the classroom. (present)
- **Next week** the school will close. (future)

**Try this:** Make a list of five more time words. Put each one under Past, Present or Future.

## The simple present

We use the **simple present** for things that happen **every day** or are **always true**.

- I **walk** to school.
- Kofi **walks** to school.
- The sun **rises** in the east.
- We **eat** jollof on Sundays.

Notice the **-s**. When we talk about **he, she or it** (one person or thing), the verb takes an **s**:

- I walk → he walk**s**
- You eat → she eat**s**
- They sing → it sing**s**

**Try this:** Fill in the gaps.
1. Ama ___ (like) mangoes.
2. The boys ___ (play) football.
3. My father ___ (drive) a trotro.
4. We ___ (go) to church on Sunday.

## Adding -s and -es

Most verbs just add **-s** for he, she or it: walks, eats, plays, sings.

But some verbs add **-es**. These are verbs that end in **-s, -sh, -ch, -x** or **-o**:

- wash → wash**es**
- watch → watch**es**
- fix → fix**es**
- go → go**es**
- do → do**es**

Verbs that end in a consonant + **y** change **y** to **ies**:

- carry → carr**ies**
- fly → fl**ies**
- study → stud**ies**

But if there is a vowel before y, just add s: play → play**s**.

**Try this:** Write the he/she form: brush, cry, kiss, buy, teach.
===
## The present continuous

The **present continuous** is for things happening **right now**, at this moment.

We make it with **am / is / are** + verb + **-ing**.

- I **am reading** a book.
- Kofi **is eating** kenkey.
- The children **are playing** outside.

Compare:
- Ama **sells** oranges. (every day, her job)
- Ama **is selling** oranges. (right now, at this moment)

**Try this:** Look around. Write three sentences about what people are doing right now. Use **is** or **are** and **-ing**.

## The simple past

We use the **simple past** for things that **already happened** and are finished.

For most verbs, we add **-ed**:

- walk → walk**ed**
- play → play**ed**
- cook → cook**ed**
- jump → jump**ed**

- Yesterday Abena **walked** to the market.
- Last Saturday we **played** football.
- My mother **cooked** banku last night.

The good news: in the past tense, the verb is the **same for everyone**. I walked, he walked, they walked.

**Try this:** Change to the past: I clean my room. Kofi kicks the ball. They watch TV.

## Spelling the -ed ending

Adding **-ed** has a few rules.

**Rule 1:** If the verb already ends in **e**, just add **d**.
- like → like**d**
- dance → dance**d**
- bake → bake**d**

**Rule 2:** If the verb ends in consonant + **y**, change **y** to **ied**.
- cry → cr**ied**
- carry → carr**ied**
- study → stud**ied**

**Rule 3:** Short verbs ending in one vowel + one consonant **double** the last letter.
- stop → sto**pped**
- plan → pla**nned**
- hop → ho**pped**

**Try this:** Write the past tense: hope, try, drop, wash, hurry.

## Regular verbs

A **regular verb** follows the rule: add **-ed** for the past.

Here are some regular verbs you use every day:

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| walk | walked |
| talk | talked |
| cook | cooked |
| wash | washed |
| help | helped |
| play | played |
| clean | cleaned |
| open | opened |
| want | wanted |
| visit | visited |

- Kofi **helped** his father on the farm.
- We **visited** Cape Coast last year.
- Ama **wanted** to buy a new pencil.

**Try this:** Write a sentence in the past tense using three of these verbs.

## Irregular verbs

Some verbs are **irregular**. They do **not** add **-ed**. They change in their own special way.

- go → **went** (not "goed")
- eat → **ate** (not "eated")
- see → **saw**
- have → **had**
- come → **came**
- take → **took**
- give → **gave**
- run → **ran**

- Yesterday I **went** to the market.
- Kofi **ate** three balls of kenkey.
- We **saw** an elephant at Mole.

There is no rule for these. You must **learn them by heart**. The more you read, the more you will remember them.

**Try this:** Cover the past column. Can you say each past form without looking?

## Verbs that do not change

A few irregular verbs look **exactly the same** in the present and past.

- put → put
- cut → cut
- hit → hit
- let → let
- read → read (but it sounds different: "reed" and "red")
- hurt → hurt

- Every day Ama **puts** her bag on the desk. (present, with -s)
- Yesterday Ama **put** her bag on the desk. (past, no -s)

How do we know the tense? Look for the **time word** and look at whether the verb has **-s**.

**Try this:** Write two sentences with "cut", one present and one past. Use a time word in each.
===
## To be: am, is, are, was, were

The verb **to be** is the most irregular verb of all.

**Present:**
- I **am**
- you **are**
- he / she / it **is**
- we / they **are**

**Past:**
- I **was**
- you **were**
- he / she / it **was**
- we / they **were**

- Today I **am** at school. Yesterday I **was** at home.
- The pupils **are** quiet now. They **were** noisy before.
- It **is** hot today. It **was** cool yesterday.

**Try this:** Fill in was or were.
1. The market ___ busy yesterday.
2. We ___ late for school.
3. My grandmother ___ a teacher.

## Telling what happened yesterday

Ama tells her class what she did yesterday:

> "Yesterday I **woke** up at six. I **swept** the compound and **fetched** water. Then I **ate** porridge and **walked** to school. After school, I **helped** my mother at the market. We **sold** all the tomatoes. In the evening I **did** my homework and **went** to bed at nine."

Count the verbs. Which are regular (fetched, walked, helped)? Which are irregular (woke, swept, ate, sold, did, went)?

## Did not, in the past

To say something did **not** happen in the past, use **did not** (or **didn't**) + the **present** form of the verb.

- I **did not go** to school. (not "did not went")
- Kofi **did not eat** his lunch. (not "did not ate")
- They **didn't play** football.

The word **did** carries the past. So the main verb goes back to its plain form.

Questions work the same way: **Did** you **go**? **Did** Ama **eat**?

- "**Did** you **see** the match?" "No, I **didn't see** it."

**Try this:** Make these negative: Ama bought bread. We went to Kumasi. He wrote a letter.
===
## The simple future

The **simple future** is for things that **have not happened yet**.

We make it with **will** + verb.

- Tomorrow I **will visit** my aunt.
- Next week we **will write** a test.
- Kofi **will be** ten years old in March.

The verb after **will** never changes. No -s, no -ed, no -ing.
- She **will go** (not "will goes")
- They **will eat** (not "will ate")

We can shorten **will** to **'ll**: I'll, you'll, she'll, we'll.

**Try this:** Change to the future: I play football. Ama cooks rice. We clean the classroom.

## Going to

Another way to talk about the future is **am / is / are going to** + verb.

- I **am going to** buy a new book.
- Abena **is going to** visit her grandmother.
- We **are going to** plant maize this season.

We often use **going to** for plans we have already decided.

- "What are you going to do on Saturday?"
- "I'm going to wash my school uniform."

**Will** and **going to** both talk about the future. Both are correct.

**Try this:** Write three plans for the weekend using "going to".
===
## Will not

To say something will **not** happen, use **will not** or **won't**.

- I **will not** be late tomorrow.
- Kofi **won't** forget his homework again.
- It **won't** rain today.

Questions use **Will** at the front:
- **Will** you come to my party?
- **Will** the trotro stop here?

Short answers: "Yes, I will." "No, I won't."

**Try this:** Answer these questions in full sentences.
1. Will you go to school tomorrow?
2. Will it be hot next week?
3. Will your class win the sports competition?

Find all the future verbs. Which use **will**? Which use **going to**? Which one is negative?

## One verb, three tenses

Here is the verb **cook** in all three tenses:

- **Past:** Yesterday my mother **cooked** jollof.
- **Present:** Every Sunday my mother **cooks** jollof.
- **Future:** Tomorrow my mother **will cook** jollof.

Here is the irregular verb **eat**:

- **Past:** Yesterday I **ate** jollof.
- **Present:** I **eat** jollof every Sunday.
- **Future:** I **will eat** jollof tomorrow.

Only the past tense changes shape for irregular verbs. The future always uses **will** + plain verb.

## Choosing the right tense

Read the time word. Then choose the tense.

1. **Yesterday** Kofi ___ (play) football. → **played** (past)
2. **Every day** Kofi ___ (play) football. → **plays** (present)
3. **Tomorrow** Kofi ___ (play) football. → **will play** (future)

Now with an irregular verb:

1. **Last week** we ___ (go) to Kumasi. → **went**
2. We ___ (go) to Kumasi **every holiday**. → **go**
3. **Next month** we ___ (go) to Kumasi. → **will go**

**Try this:** Choose the tense.
1. Last night I ___ (see) a film.
2. My sister ___ (sing) in the choir every Sunday.
3. Next year I ___ (be) in Basic 5.

## Keeping the same tense

When you tell a story, **stay in the same tense**. Do not jump between past and present.

**Wrong:** Yesterday I went to the market. I buy some oranges. Then I walk home.

**Right:** Yesterday I **went** to the market. I **bought** some oranges. Then I **walked** home.

The first version is confusing. Did it happen yesterday or today?

If your story is about yesterday, keep every verb in the past. If it is about tomorrow, keep every verb in the future.

**Try this:** Fix this story so every verb is past tense:
"Last Sunday we visit my uncle. He gives us rice and stew. We play with our cousins and come home at six."

## Asking questions in each tense

**Present:** Use **Do** or **Does**.
- **Do** you like rice?
- **Does** Kofi walk to school?

**Past:** Use **Did**.
- **Did** you like the film?
- **Did** Kofi walk to school yesterday?

**Future:** Use **Will**.
- **Will** you like the new teacher?
- **Will** Kofi walk to school tomorrow?

After do, does, did or will, the main verb stays in its **plain form**: like, walk.

**Try this:** Write one question in each tense to ask your friend about food.
===
## Question words

Question words go at the front: **What, Where, When, Who, Why, How**.

- **What did** you eat yesterday?
- **Where does** your uncle live?
- **When will** the rain stop?
- **Who did** you see at the market?
- **Why are** you late?
- **How will** we get to Kumasi?

The tense is still shown by do/does, did or will.

**Try this:** Write three questions to ask a friend about last weekend. Write three about next weekend. Use different question words.
===
## Was doing: the past continuous

Sometimes we want to say something **was happening** in the past, for a while.

We use **was / were** + verb + **-ing**.

- At six o'clock, I **was sweeping** the yard.
- The children **were playing** when it started to rain.
- Ama **was reading** while Kofi **was cooking**.

Compare:
- I **swept** the yard. (finished, simple past)
- I **was sweeping** the yard when my friend came. (in the middle of it)

**Try this:** What were you doing at 7 o'clock this morning? Write two sentences using "was" or "were" + -ing.
===
## Have and has

The verb **have** is irregular.

**Present:** I have, you have, he/she/it **has**, we have, they have.
**Past:** **had** for everyone.
**Future:** will have.

- Ama **has** two brothers.
- Yesterday we **had** rice for lunch.
- Tomorrow we **will have** a test.

**Has / have** can also help other verbs: "I **have finished** my work." This means it is done now.

**Try this:** Fill in have, has or had.
1. Kofi ___ a new bag today.
2. Last year I ___ a bicycle.
3. They ___ three goats.
===
## Do and does

The verb **do** is irregular too.

**Present:** I do, you do, he/she/it **does**, we do, they do.
**Past:** **did**.
**Future:** will do.

- I **do** my homework every evening.
- Abena **does** her homework after dinner.
- Yesterday I **did** my homework early.
- Tomorrow I **will do** it before dinner.

Remember, do/does/did also help make questions and negatives: "**Do** you...?" "She **doesn't**..." "We **didn't**..."

**Try this:** Write three sentences about homework, one in each tense, using do.
===
## Common mistakes

**Mistake 1:** Adding -ed to irregular verbs.
"I goed." ✗ → "I **went**." ✓
"She eated." ✗ → "She **ate**." ✓

**Mistake 2:** Two past markers.
"I did not went." ✗ → "I did not **go**." ✓

**Mistake 3:** Forgetting -s in the present.
"Kofi walk to school." ✗ → "Kofi walk**s** to school." ✓

**Mistake 4:** Changing the verb after will.
"She will goes." ✗ → "She will **go**." ✓

**Mistake 5:** Mixing tenses in a story.
"Yesterday I go and buy..." ✗ → "Yesterday I **went** and **bought**..." ✓

**Try this:** Find and fix the mistake: "Last week Ama buyed a dress and she will wears it on Sunday."

## Tense in Ghanaian languages

In Twi, Ga, Ewe and other Ghanaian languages, tense works differently. Often a short word before the verb shows the time.

For example, in Twi:
- **Mekɔ** = I go (present)
- **Mekɔɔ** = I went (past)
- **Mɛkɔ** = I will go (future)

The idea is the same: the verb changes a little to show **when**. English uses **-ed**, **will**, and irregular forms. Your language has its own way.

**Try this:** Ask a parent or elder how to say "I ate", "I eat" and "I will eat" in your home language. Write them down.

## Words to remember

- **Verb**: a doing or being word.
- **Tense**: the form of the verb that shows **when**.
- **Present tense**: now, or every day. *walks, eats*
- **Past tense**: already happened. *walked, ate*
- **Future tense**: not yet happened. *will walk, will eat*
- **Regular verb**: adds **-ed** in the past. *cook → cooked*
- **Irregular verb**: changes in its own way. *go → went*
- **Time word**: a word like yesterday, today, tomorrow that shows the tense.
- **Continuous**: happening for a while. *is eating, was eating*

**Try this:** Cover the meanings. Can you explain each word to a friend?
